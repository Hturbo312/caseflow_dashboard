Circle East District

Published Monday, December 1, 2025

ABOUT THE PROJECT

Project Type: Infill redevelopment transforming non-productive land into a sustainable community

Location: East Cleveland, OH

Website: Circle East District

 

Download Full Case Study as PDF

PROJECT TEAM

Developer: Cuyahoga County Land Reutilization Corporation (CCLRC)

Architect: DERU Landscape Architecture

Arborist: Bartlett Tree Experts

Verifier: Steve Armstrong, NGBS Green Master Verifier, Performance Point LLC, and William Sanderson, NGBS Green PRO Consultant, TEAM WS LLC

FROM THE DEVELOPER

“Given our determination to make sustainability a cornerstone of this redevelopment, we only sought builders who were willing and interested in embracing sustainable construction methods and alternative energy. Our builders do understand the green message.”

- Kamla Lewis Senior Housing Development Project Manager CCLRC

Overview

Circle East District in East Cleveland, Ohio—adjacent to Little Italy, University Circle, and major educational institutions—is slated for 200 townhomes and single-family homes featuring onsite renewable energy and EV charging. Led by the Cuyahoga County Land Reutilization Corporation, this transformational infill project has earned NGBS Green Land Development Certification and will also include retail space and a one-acre park.

Green Features & Practices

  • Remodeling existing homes and building new homes on underutilized land classifies this as an infill and greyfield site
  • Training of onsite supervisors and team members on pertinent green development practices
  • Agreements between the four developers to construct buildings in compliance with above code third-party green building certification program
  • Plan to protect and maintain priority natural resources/areas during construction; on-site native or regionally appropriate trees and shrubs conserved, maintained and reused for landscaping
  • Vegetative wind breaks or channels designed for local conditions
  • Existing buildings reused or adapted where possible or otherwise disassembled with building materials being reused or recycled
  • Damage to designated existing trees and vegetation mitigated during construction; soil improved with organic amendments and mulch
  • Common or public spaces within 1/4 mile walk to 80% of units
  • Dedicated bicycle parking and racks
  • Above-average access to employment within 45-minute drive


About Circle East District

East Circle District was established more than a century ago as one of the first ring suburbs of East Cleveland, Ohio. Since 1970 the city's population has declined by over 60%, driven by white flight and the foreclosure crisis. Decades of disinvestment and redlining have further compounded these challenges, leaving East Cleveland among the poorest cities in not only Ohio, but the nation.

The goal of this transformational infill redevelopment project is to reduce widespread issues of vacancy and abandonment in the area, while rebuilding the devastated tax base. This project is the result of extensive community studies conducted in 2015 and 2020, which identified the most suitable location to initiate redevelopment efforts. Ground was broken on the homes in March 2025. The neighborhood will contain new wide sidewalks and tree lined streets, new underground infrastructure, and a one-acre linear park dotted with solar pavilions following a bike path.

Home buyers will receive 100% tax abatements on the increased property value of their homes for 15 years. This can add up to $150,000 in savings.

It is estimated that this development will add $60 million in new property value and $12 million in new household income. A portion of this can be attributed to the renovation of the notable 25,000 sq. ft. Mickey’s building, which will be bought by Verdynt Biotech and will create 100 tech jobs. It is the hope that this redevelopment will serve as a catalyst for the rest of the struggling neighborhoods nearby and attract employment opportunities to revitalize East Cleveland and Cleveland, OH.

Certified Land Development

The NGBS provides menus of ANSI-approved, consensus-based green practices for planning, designing, and constructing residential and mixed-use land developments that help reduce environmental impacts and support developers' ESG goals. Any community is eligible for independent land development certification so long as some parcels will eventually include residential buildings. Certification is awarded at One- through Four-Stars, based on the points awarded beyond the minimum threshold.
