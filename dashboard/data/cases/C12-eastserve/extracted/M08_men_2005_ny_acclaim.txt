Eastserve acclaimed . . . in New York
THE EASTSERVE internet service provider for East Manchester has gained international acclaim.

THE EASTSERVE internet service provider for East Manchester has gained international acclaim.
The project, aiming to get local people online, was featured in the New York Times last month.
In an article by American reporter Mark Landler, the scheme was praised for successfully giving residents the opportunity to surf the web.
Mr Landler described Eastserve as "a virtual passport out of the decaying industrial landscape of East Manchester".
Eastserve uses wireless fidelity technology, or Wi-Fi, which offers high speed internet access in specific areas without the use of telephone sockets.
Mr Landler said: "In Manchester, the once grimy Victorian city famous for the birthplace of the Industrial Revolution, Wi-Fi is being used, for the first time ever on this scale, to bridge the industrial divide."
Radio transmitters and wireless equipment provided by Cisco Systems has allowed six-square-miles of East Manchester to access the internet using Wi-Fi technology.
This is one of the biggest schemes of its kind in Europe, and comparable projects in the US are exclusive due to high costs.
The New York Times also documented the logistical difficulties of setting up the equipment in the area.
Antennae have been set up on the rooftops of apartment blocks, and a radio signal is beamed across East Manchester. But due to the different building sizes it has been difficult for some places to receive a signal strong enough to access the internet.
Concerns were also raised in the article about the cost of the project, and how long it will receive funding for.
Mr Landler is positive about the scheme though, as he discussed the "palpable social effect the internet has had on the economically downtrodden people of East Manchester".
Principle regeneration officer for New Deal, Sean McGonigle, who helped build the EastServe network, spoke to the New York times about the project. He said that Eastserve had been "integral in getting the residents of East Manchester hooked up to the internet".
He went on to say: "The Eastserve website provides lots of useful information to the people who live here. I am extremely proud of the fact that our project has attracted interest and comment from such a prestigious and internationally renowned publication as the New York Times.
"The journalist, Mark Landler, was extremely impressed by the services provided and the direct impact it had had on residents' lives."
